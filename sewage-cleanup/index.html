A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across Springfield, Springfield Restoration Team answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.
