How to Prepare for Flooding in Springfield, IL

Home / Blog / Flood Prevention for Homeowners in Springfield, IL

While you can't control the weather, you can take proactive steps to protect your Springfield, IL property from flood damage. A prepared home takes far less of a hit when it floods, and the savings in restoration costs can reach the thousands. Below, our Springfield Restoration Team team lays out what Springfield homeowners can do to prepare most effectively.

Understand the Flood Risk You Face

Understanding your property's flood risk is the first step in preparation. FEMA publishes flood maps that show whether a designated flood zone includes your property. Even if you're not in a high-risk zone, consider that most flood claims come from outside designated flood zones. Look at how your land slopes, how it drains, and how close it sits to any waterway that might overflow in heavy rain.

Physical Steps to Protect Your Home

On the physical side, keep a sump pump with a backup battery installed and maintained, store valuables and key documents higher than ground level, grade the lot to fall away from the foundation, lift electrical panels and HVAC units above where floodwater could reach, seal any foundation cracks and add a waterproof coating, and clean gutters and downspouts while running the discharge out to 6 feet or farther from the foundation.

Insurance Preparation

Standard homeowner's insurance does not cover flood damage from rising water. Flood policies are sold by the NFIP and by private carriers, and they usually come with a 30-day wait before coverage begins, so look into one long before any forecast forces the issue. For insurance purposes, take video and photos of the property and what you own. Store your policy paperwork where you can get to it, and learn your deductible and coverage limits.

Emergency Response Plan

Have a plan in place before flooding threatens. Pack a go-bag and learn the routes you would take to evacuate. Keep contact details for your insurance agent somewhere easy to find. If flooding is forecast, move valuables to upper levels, turn off utilities if instructed, and document your property's condition before the event.
