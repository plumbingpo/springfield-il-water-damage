Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Springfield

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Do not wait for the water to drain away by itself.
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Wash and Disinfect

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Discharge piping that has come apart, been flattened or plugged up outdoors.
  • Stuck or snagged float switches, which never tell the pump to start.
  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Finished Basement Walls

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Carpet and Flooring

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Water depth, along with the square footage that flooded.
  • The time that passed before the water was pumped out and drying started.
  • How many stored belongings have to be moved, cleaned or thrown away.
  • If water is still coming in, the cost of holding it back with a temporary pump.
