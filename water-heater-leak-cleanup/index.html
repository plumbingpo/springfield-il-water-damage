Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.

Coverage

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Time that passed with the tank still refilling, up to the point the supply was shut.
  • Quantity of insulation and drywall, on walls or ceilings, that must come out.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
  • Condition of the stand or subfloor below the heater, which may need rebuilding before the replacement is set.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.

What Happens if You Wait

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Garage Wall Base

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

Heater Closet Drywall

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.
