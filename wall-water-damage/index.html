Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Springfield

Drywall acts like a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Mold Inside Cavities

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.
