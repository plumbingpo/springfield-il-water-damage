Bathroom Water Damage in Springfield

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. Our Springfield team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Warning Signs

  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Slow Vanity Drips

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.
